Awwab Fitness — Nutrition

Weight gain

Your calorie target comes from your personal calculator, which starts at your maintenance estimate and steps up gradually each week. This page covers how to actually build meals around that number.

Your calorie number

Weeks 1–2 hold flat at your estimated maintenance so your coach can confirm the real number against how your body actually responds. From Week 3 on, your target steps up by 100 kcal each week — a gradual surplus that supports lean gains without piling on unnecessary fat.

Your macro formula

This is the same formula used across weight loss, weight gain, and recomp — only your total calorie number changes between goals, not the macro split:

  • Protein: 0.8g per lb of bodyweight — fixed for the full 12 weeks
  • Fat: 0.3g per lb of bodyweight — fixed for the full 12 weeks
  • Carbs: fill whatever calories remain after protein and fat

On a gain, carbs are what grows week to week as your total calories step up — that's what fuels the training-to-failure effort this program calls for on a surplus. Your exact numbers are in your calorie calculator.

Sourcing your food based on your situation

The macro targets above are the same for everyone. Which foods you use to hit them depends on your intake answer about grocery budget — ask your coach if you're not sure which applies to you.

Full flexibility

Comfortable grocery budget

  • Protein: chicken, beef, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt
  • Carbs: rice, oats, potatoes, fruit, whole grains
  • Basic macro tracking helps — surplus eating is easy to under-shoot without it
Some budget flexibility

Working with a moderate budget

  • Budget-friendly protein: eggs, chicken thighs, canned tuna, beans
  • Budget-friendly carbs: rice, oats, potatoes, peanut butter
  • Add an extra snack or shake between meals if you're struggling to eat enough overall
Limited budget

Keeping it simple and realistic

  • Reliable cheap protein and carbs: eggs, rice, oats, peanut butter, potatoes
  • Don't skip meals — eating enough total volume matters more than precision here
  • Consistency in training and eating enough matters more than exact numbers at this stage
Track your nutrition (1–5) and exercise intensity in your daily check-in. If your nutrition score is consistently low, that's a sign the current guidance may not be realistic for your life — not a reason to force it.
