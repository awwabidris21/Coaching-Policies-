Awwab Fitness — Nutrition

Weight loss

Your calorie target comes from your personal calculator, which starts at your maintenance estimate and steps down gradually each week. This page covers how to actually build meals around that number.

Your calorie number

Weeks 1–2 hold flat at your estimated maintenance so your coach can confirm the real number against how your body actually responds. From Week 3 on, your target steps down by 100 kcal each week — a gradual, sustainable pace rather than a sharp cut.

Your macro formula

This is the same formula used across weight loss, weight gain, and recomp — only your total calorie number changes between goals, not the macro split:

  • Protein: 0.8g per lb of bodyweight — fixed for the full 12 weeks
  • Fat: 0.3g per lb of bodyweight — fixed for the full 12 weeks
  • Carbs: fill whatever calories remain after protein and fat

On a cut, carbs are what actually shrinks week to week as your total calories step down — protein and fat stay constant so you hold onto muscle and keep hormones/energy stable while losing fat. Your exact numbers are in your calorie calculator.

Sourcing your food based on your situation

The macro targets above are the same for everyone. Which foods you use to hit them depends on your intake answer about grocery budget — ask your coach if you're not sure which applies to you.

Full flexibility

Comfortable grocery budget

  • Lean proteins: chicken breast, lean beef, fish, egg whites
  • Carb sources: rice, oats, potatoes, fruit
  • Basic macro tracking helps you hit the protein/fat/carb targets precisely
Some budget flexibility

Working with a moderate budget

  • Budget-friendly protein: eggs, canned tuna, chicken thighs, beans, lentils
  • Budget-friendly carbs: rice, oats, potatoes
  • Portion by hand-size if exact tracking isn't realistic: palm of protein, cupped hand of carbs
Limited budget

Keeping it simple and realistic

  • Reliable cheap protein: eggs, frozen chicken, canned fish, beans
  • Reliable cheap carbs: rice, oats, potatoes
  • Hit protein first at each meal, then fill the rest with carbs — don't stress the exact gram count
Track your nutrition (1–5) and exercise intensity in your daily check-in. If your nutrition score is consistently low, that's a sign to revisit whether your current guidance actually fits your life — not a reason to push harder.
