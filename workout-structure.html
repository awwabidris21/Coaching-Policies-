Workout structure

This is the same training framework whether your goal is weight loss, weight gain, or recomp — the difference between those goals lives in your nutrition, not your training.

Core principles

  • Full-body focusMore frequent practice of each movement pattern builds skill faster than splitting body parts too early — this applies at every training frequency.
  • Compound movements firstSquats, hinges, pushes, pulls, and carries do the most work for your time — these anchor every session.
  • Lift heavy — 3 sets of 6–8 repsThe default range for main lifts. Heavier weight, lower reps builds strength efficiently and holds up well across all three goals.
  • Effort depends on your goalSee the effort guide below — it's not the same across weight loss, weight gain, and recomp.
  • Always warm up5 minutes of light cardio plus bodyweight versions of that day's main movements, every session.

Effort by goal

Weight loss

Train near failure, not to failure — leave about 1 rep in the tank (RIR 1) on your main lifts. You're managing recovery capacity on a calorie deficit, so pushing every set to failure adds fatigue you can't fully recover from.

Weight gain & recomp

Train to failure on your main lifts. You have the calories (and for recomp, the protein) to support full recovery, so push each working set as far as it goes.

Pick your movements — then stick with them

In your first week, you'll try a few options for each movement pattern (squat, press, row, hinge, etc.) and pick the ones that feel right for your body. Once you've picked, you stick with those same exercises for the full 12 weeks. This is intentional — real strength comes from consistent practice on the same lifts, not constantly switching things up.

Frequency & structure

Your training days per week are built around your actual schedule — not a fixed number everyone follows. Below are the three most common structures. Every structure is designed so each major muscle group — including legs and abs — gets trained twice a week.

Cardio isn't a separate training day in this program — it's a daily 10,000 step target, tracked alongside your workouts.

3 days/week — full body

Rotation

Alternate Workout A and Workout B each session (A-B-A one week, B-A-B the next). Abs live in Workout A only — since A repeats twice in a normal week, that lands abs at 2x/week automatically.

  • Squat pattern
  • Push pattern (press)
  • Pull pattern (row)
  • Hinge pattern (deadlift variation)
  • Abs (Workout A only)

4 days/week — upper/lower split

Two lower-body days, two upper-body days per week, with the same movement patterns above split by body region instead of trained together. Abs are programmed into both Upper days — since Upper runs twice a week, abs land at 2x/week.

5 days/week — Upper/Lower/Push/Pull/Legs

Upper, Lower, Push, Pull, Legs — in that order. This structure naturally hits every major muscle group twice: Upper covers chest/back/shoulders early in the week, then Push and Pull hit those same muscles again later from a different angle. Lower and Legs do the same for the lower body.

Abs are programmed into Pull day and Upper day — landing at 2x/week.

Equipment adapts, structure doesn't

Whether you're training in a full gym, at home with dumbbells, or with no equipment at all, the movement patterns stay the same — only the specific exercise changes (e.g. barbell squat → goblet squat → bodyweight squat). Your coach will pick the version that matches what you have access to.

How you'll progress

Double progression: once you hit 8 reps for all 3 sets with good form, the weight goes up next session and you drop back to 6 reps. Repeat.

No weight available? Progress by adding reps, slowing down the lowering phase, or increasing time under tension instead.
